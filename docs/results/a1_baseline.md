# Agents-A1-35B as a matched competitor baseline (dev set, grep and semantic arms)

Run 2026-10-27 (21:42 to 21:56 PT). Agents-A1-35B, the competing model, was run through the same eval harness, tasks, prompt, tools, budgets and scorer as the wayfinder-glimmer and v4 retain-on arms. It is clearly behind both on reported recall: wayfinder-glimmer minus A1 is +0.098 [+0.055, +0.143] on the grep arm and +0.066 [+0.018, +0.119] on the semantic arm, and v4 minus A1 is +0.111 [+0.065, +0.159] and +0.061 [+0.009, +0.119]. A1 does not converge: 54% of its grep rollouts end by `report`, the median is 38 turns, and it uses about 785k tokens per rollout against 269k for wayfinder-glimmer. Against the PRD section 15 criteria it passes 2 of 5. Every Wayfinder checkpoint that clears wayfinder-glimmer by a margin also clears A1; the practical bar for A1 is a reported recall of about 0.31 (grep) and 0.33 (sem).

## What was run

`wayfinder/rollout/a1_eval_app.py` is a copy of `sft_eval_app.py` (not an edit of it, because the running GRPO drivers launch that file). It serves `/weights/Agents-A1-FP8` with vLLM 0.32.0 on one H100 per arm and runs the rollouts in the same container, as every earlier eval did. I chose this over the deployed `wayfinder-a1-35b` endpoint because that endpoint is capped at one container and 32 concurrent requests behind a bearer proxy and is kept for the demo; four private H100 containers were faster, the rollouts stay next to the server (the laptop cannot resolve `*.modal.host`), and no secret was needed. The deployed endpoint was not touched and was not woken (0 tasks throughout), so there was nothing to scale back to zero.

What is identical to the wayfinder-glimmer and v4 retain-on arms: the 50 dev tasks times seeds 0 and 1 (`artifacts/teacher_test/stageB_tasks.json`, split into two 25-task shards that run in parallel), scout's product prompt with the convergence nudge (`run.TEACHER_NUDGE`), grep, glob, read_file and report (plus `semantic_search` in the `sem` arm, `semantic=auto`), `max_turns` 40, `max_seconds` 3600, `retain_reasoning` on, max_model_len 65536, the same row schema and `score_row`. What differs on purpose is the model and its serving contract: A1's own vLLM flags from `serve/a1_35b.py` (`qwen3_coder` tool parser, `qwen3` reasoning parser, FP8 as shipped, no `--dtype`), and A1's recommended sampling, sent on every request through scout's `a1` profile: temperature 0.85, top_p 0.95, top_k 20, presence_penalty 1.1, `chat_template_kwargs.enable_thinking` true. The Wayfinder arms use temperature 0.85 and top_p 0.95 only, so the temperature and top_p match and the other three settings are A1's. Two serving differences are not model properties: `--max-num-seqs` is 32 (A1's setting) instead of 64, and concurrency was 25 per container instead of 14, so wall time and per-turn latency are not comparable across the models.

Four containers (two shards times two arms) ran at once, within the 4 H100 limit. Each loaded in 255 to 264 s and finished its arm in 458 to 557 s. A startup check (model served as `a1`, a tool call parses) passed on all four. Watcher: `ops/a1_eval_watch.py` (prefix `wayfinder-a1eval`, 150-minute kill age, auth canary), armed before the first launch. No `wayfinder-a1eval*` app is live.

## Row validation

Both arms have 100 rows, 50 tasks matching the dev file, each (task, seed) once, seeds {0, 1}, 0 errors, model `a1` on every row, `retain_reasoning` true, sampling T0.85 / top_p0.95 / top_k20 / presence_penalty1.1 / thinking true, max_turns 40, max_seconds 3600, no 429s and no retries. Turns min / median / max are 8 / 38 / 40 (grep) and 7 / 39 / 40 (sem); wall time median 200 s and 202 s. Every row carries reasoning content. Rows: `artifacts/a1_baseline/rows/sft_a1_{grep,sem}.jsonl.gz` (the plain `sft_a1_{grep,sem}.jsonl` and the shard files are regenerated by `scripts/score_a1_baseline.py` and are not committed). Full validation per arm is in `artifacts/a1_baseline/summary.json`.

## Tool-call health

Counted from the rows, not hidden. A1's `qwen3_coder` parser produced a parsed tool call whenever the model emitted one: no `<tool_call>` markup leaked into message content on any turn, no bad-JSON arguments, and no empty turns. The failures are at the schema level, which scout's loop counts as malformed calls. Grep arm: 3,695 tool calls over 3,323 assistant turns, 30 malformed (0.81% of calls): 21 invalid arguments (10 `grep` with `context` above the maximum of 5, 4 `read_file` with a non-integer `end_line`, 3 `report` with `gaps` not a list, 2 `read_file` with an invented `stop_line`, 2 other), 9 calls to `semantic_search`, a tool the grep arm does not offer. Six turns carried no tool call (0.18% of turns), which triggers scout's "Reply with a tool call" nudge; 5 rollouts saw it. 22 of 100 rollouts had at least one malformed call and none was stopped for three in a row. Sem arm: 3,554 calls, 14 malformed (0.39%: 13 invalid arguments, 1 call to a tool named `search`), 2 turns without a tool call, 11 rollouts touched, and one rollout was stopped by scout after three consecutive malformed calls. For comparison the wayfinder-glimmer and v4 arms have 0.07% to 0.11% malformed calls and at most one no-call turn. A1's malformed rate is about 4 to 12 times higher but small in absolute terms, and it does not explain the 54% report rate: the turn cap is what ends the other 43 rollouts.

## Does A1's template render earlier reasoning?

Yes, on the path the harness uses. A1's chat template (`chat_template.jinja`) renders `message.reasoning_content` inside `<think>` for assistant turns after the last user message, which covers every turn of a tool loop. vLLM 0.32.0's chat endpoint renames an incoming `reasoning_content` to `reasoning` and the message parser sets both fields for the template, and the template reads `reasoning_content`. Two checks agree. A CPU render of the template shows both reasoning markers when the field is `reasoning_content` and neither when it is `reasoning` alone (`artifacts/a1_baseline/template_check.json`, a template-level check that does not exercise vLLM's request handling). On the real rollouts, the prompt grows between consecutive turns by about the previous turn's output tokens: for the 188 turn pairs with at least 300 output tokens and a short tool result, the median growth is 1.05 times the output size and 99% of pairs grow by at least 0.8 times it; if reasoning were dropped, growth would be a small fraction of the output (the reasoning is most of it). So retain-reasoning is effective for A1, not a no-op. One caveat is a false negative in the startup probe: it rendered through vLLM's `/tokenize` and showed no reasoning for either the retained or the stripped input (probably because that endpoint does not take the same message path as chat completions, which I did not confirm); that probe result (`meta.adapter_check.template_check` in the shard meta files) should not be read as the template dropping reasoning. One template property is real and unrelated to this harness: after a plain user message (the loop's nudge when a model emits no tool call), earlier reasoning is dropped by design; this happened in 7 rollouts.

## Reading and caveats

A1 is a weaker retrieval agent than wayfinder-glimmer and v4 under this harness, mainly because it keeps exploring: the reported-recall gap (+0.10 on grep) comes with a 24-turn and 500k-token gap, and the 54% report rate means a large part of A1's score is budget-fallback credit. This is the scout product loop with A1's own sampling and parser, which is what the author asked for, and it replaces the earlier "not comparable" A1 numbers (benchmark harness, k5 view: 0.48 for the g arm) in `docs/teacher_test.md`. It is still one A1 configuration: A1's turn-cap behaviour might change with a different nudge or a larger turn budget, and the 40-turn cap is the Wayfinder protocol, not A1's. The tasks are the 50 dev tasks (18 repos), tasks are the bootstrap unit, A1 has one seeded sample per (task, seed), and the Wayfinder references are single training runs. A1 ran at concurrency 25 and `--max-num-seqs 32`, so timings are not comparable. FP8 weights as shipped were used, not bf16.

## Wiring into GRPO scoring

`scripts/score_grpo.py` now loads A1 as a fourth reference (`artifacts/a1_baseline/sft_a1_{grep,sem}.jsonl`, regenerated from the committed gz by `python3 scripts/score_a1_baseline.py`). Every healthy checkpoint arm gets `vs_a1` paired deltas (checkpoint minus A1, same arm) in `artifacts/grpo/summary.json`, and the printed output has a `vs wayfinder-glimmer` line and a `vs A1` line (reported recall, all-gold, touched recall, turns, tokens), flagged when the reported-recall CI lower bound does not clear zero. If the A1 rows are missing it prints n/a instead of failing. Tested on the smoke checkpoint (read from a copy; the real `artifacts/grpo` outputs were not changed). Not touched: the running `wayfinder-grpo-smoke` apps and their drivers.
